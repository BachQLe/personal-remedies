# Health-claim audit (checklist #65)

Prepared 4 Oct 2026 for Mory and Sunny. This is a copy review against the app store policies, not legal advice. Every row below was read in the code on this date; line numbers will drift as other people edit, so search for the quoted text if a line number is off.

## How to read this

Remedi ranks foods against a person's conditions using Nutridigm's ratings and shows them as stars, skulls and words like "Top", "Helps" and "Avoid". Neither Apple nor Google bans that. What both stores react to is wording that says or implies that a food treats, cures, prevents or heals a disease, wording that claims clinical proof the app cannot back, and a missing reminder to talk to a doctor.

Each row has a severity:

- **High**: states or clearly implies a treat/cure/prevent claim, or a proof claim. Fix before submission.
- **Medium**: could be read as a medical claim by a strict reviewer. Fix or get Sunny's sign-off.
- **Low**: probably fine, listed so the decision is deliberate.

Two groups of rows:

- **Group A** is text that ships in the app or the app's metadata today.
- **Group B** is marketing-website code in `src/pages/marketing/**` and `src/components/marketing/**`. The routes for those pages are removed (commit `cd258eb`, "Hide marketing site"), so users cannot reach them. Only `/login`, `/terms` and `/privacy` are still routed. Group B is still in the repo, and Apple and Google both review the developer's linked website and marketing URL, so these need fixing or deleting before the site is ever restored or linked from a store listing.

## Policies cited (current text read on 4 Oct 2026)

- **Apple 1.4.1, Medical apps**: apps that "could provide inaccurate data or information, or that could be used for diagnosing or treating patients may be reviewed with greater scrutiny." Apps "must clearly disclose data and methodology to support accuracy claims" and "should remind users to check with a doctor in addition to using the app and before making medical decisions." https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#physical-harm
- **Apple 5.1.1 (data collection, privacy policy, account deletion), 5.1.2 and 5.1.3 (health data)**: the same page, https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage and https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#health-and-health-research. 5.1.3 restricts using health data for advertising or data mining. Remedi does neither today (no ad or analytics SDK in `package.json`). The data-handling gaps are in the companion document `docs/privacy-terms-gap-review.md`.
- **Apple 2.3.1(a), accurate metadata**: marketing the app "in a misleading way, such as by promoting content or services that it does not actually offer" is grounds for removal. https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#accurate-metadata
- **Google Play, Health Content and Services**: forbids "apps with health and medical related functionalities that are misleading or potentially harmful"; non-medical health apps need a disclaimer in the app description that the app "is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition"; and all health apps should remind users to consult a healthcare professional. https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16679511
- **Google Play, Unapproved Substances (misleading health claims)**: prohibits false or misleading health claims, and products "marketed in a way that implies that they're safe or effective for use in preventing, curing, or treating a particular disease or ailment." Nutridigm includes herbals and nutrients (food group "k"), so supplement-style claims are in scope. Summary of the policy: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16555673

Google's Play listing also requires the "Health apps declaration" in Play Console, which is separate from the copy and is covered in `docs/store-listing-draft.md`.

## What is already fine

- **Medical disclaimer content.** `src/screens/profile/importantNotesContent.js` says Remedi "is not medical advice, does not diagnose or treat any condition, and its publishers are not practicing medicine"; "Always consult your physician or a qualified health professional before changing your diet, especially if you have a medical condition or take medications"; and gives an emergency line. This satisfies the wording of Apple 1.4.1's reminder and Google's "consult a healthcare professional" requirement. The weak point is placement, not wording (see the first gap below).
- **Non-food guidance.** `src/data/nonFoodGuidance.js` (smoking, alcohol, activity, sleep, stress, therapies, medicines) is clean. Every block ends with "General information only, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor before making changes…", the medicine block says "this app does not give dosing advice", and the quit-smoking and poison-help lines point to real public hotlines. No treat/cure/prevent language. Sunny still needs to approve it, as the file header says.
- **Printed plan footer.** `planExport.js:23` and `MealQueueScreen.jsx:1261`: "Generated by Remedi — not medical advice."
- **Nutrient and USDA copy.** `NutrientFactsPanel.jsx`, `NutrientSources.jsx`, `NutritionFactsSheet.jsx` ("Rough estimates by food group — not measured values.", "Nutrients USDA did not report … are not shown.") are factual and appropriately hedged.
- **Home announcement.** `public/messages.json`, "Top recipes matched to your profile — tap to browse." is clean.
- **Onboarding.** `RemediWelcome.jsx` ("Eating for your health can feel overwhelming. Find foods that fit your needs, one small step at a time.") and `ProfileBuilder.jsx` ("Select the health topics that matter most to you") make no claim. The phrase "Show my top foods!" from the checklist is not in the code any more; the button now reads "Continue".
- **Paywall.** `PaywallScreen.jsx` contains no health claim and says plainly "Not a real purchase".
- **Error and empty states** across `DataState.jsx`, `ErrorBoundary.jsx` and the screens are neutral.
- **Honest-data handling.** The app shows "No data for this item" rather than inventing a rating, and recipes never show ingredient or study claims. That supports the 1.4.1 accuracy position.

## Gaps that are not wording

1. **The doctor reminder is one tap deep.** The Medical disclaimer lives only at Profile, then Important notes (`/app/profile/important-notes`). Nothing in onboarding, on the food detail card, or on the ranking screens carries it. Apple 1.4.1 says apps "should remind users to check with a doctor in addition to using the app and before making medical decisions." Recommended: a one-line, always-visible reminder on the Suggestions and food detail screens, and an acknowledgement step before the conditions step of onboarding (the same recommendation already made in `docs/terms-consent-research.md`).
2. **Google needs the disclaimer in the store description.** Google says non-medical health apps must say in the app description that the app "is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition." The draft description in `docs/store-listing-draft.md` includes this sentence.
3. **Condition names themselves.** Users pick diseases (diabetes, hypertension and so on). Showing a disease name next to "Helps" or "Beneficial" is what creates the claim (A7, A10). Changing the verbs to "ranked favorably" removes most of the risk without changing the product.
4. **Herbals and nutrients (Nutridigm group "k").** The app can show herbal and supplement items ranked for a condition. Google's Unapproved Substances policy cares about promotion and sale. Remedi neither sells nor links to buy supplements today, which is fine; keep it that way, and keep the Terms line (`TermsOfUse.jsx:81`) "We do not advertise any third party product".
